# Traffic Light Method (Método del Semáforo)

## Overview

The **Traffic Light Method** is the pedagogical methodology that accompanies JULIUS. JULIUS should therefore be understood not simply as software or a chatbot, but as an AI educational assistant operating inside a structured learning and assessment method.

The methodology guides learners and instructors to analyze a programming problem before evaluating or producing pseudocode. Its central structure is the identification and validation of:

1. **Inputs** — data required to solve the problem.
2. **Processes** — operations, decisions, calculations and transformations applied to those data.
3. **Outputs** — results that the algorithm must produce.

This Input–Process–Output structure is used as a scaffold for logical and algorithmic reasoning.

## Evolution in the research line

### EDUNINE 2025 — Structured seven-step methodology

The 2025 workshop, *Classroom digital transformation: chatbots as Virtual Tutors to strengthen logical and algorithmic thinking in Engineering students*, describes JULIUS as a chatbot used with a **seven-step process**. The methodology guides students in identifying algorithmic elements such as input and output variables, conditions and calculations before solving the problem.

The chatbot provides immediate and personalized feedback. The workshop also examines cognitive, pedagogical, social and emotional dimensions through qualitative and quantitative analysis.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/EDUNINE62377.2025.10981418

### FIE 2025 — Empirical evaluation in an authentic educational experience

The FIE 2025 research-to-practice paper reports a real educational experience using JULIUS as an AI-powered virtual tutor in programming education. The study uses a mixed-methods approach with quantitative pre- and post-tests and qualitative thematic analysis.

The public bibliographic record reports significant improvements in conceptual understanding, logical reasoning, motivation and collaboration, together with gains in emotional well-being, metacognition and teamwork.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/FIE63693.2025.11328240

### EDUNINE 2026 — Formalization as the Traffic Light Method

The 2026 workshop explicitly describes JULIUS as using a structured methodology based on the **"traffic light method"**. JULIUS analyzes **inputs, processes and outputs before evaluating pseudocode**, with the aim of reducing subjectivity, improving error traceability and increasing teacher efficiency.

The system produces personalized reports, group-performance indicators and identifies error patterns, supporting targeted pedagogical interventions and transforming assessment into a formative process.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/EDUNINE62390.2026.11546813

## Pedagogical principle

The method follows a simple rule:

> **Understand the problem before judging the code.**

The AI does not replace the teacher. JULIUS organizes evidence, proposes feedback and highlights patterns. The instructor reviews the analysis and makes the final academic decision.

## Suggested interpretation of the traffic-light metaphor

The traffic-light metaphor may be used to communicate the status of the student's reasoning:

- **Green:** the element is correctly identified or sufficiently justified.
- **Yellow:** the element is partially correct, incomplete or requires revision.
- **Red:** the element is missing, inconsistent or prevents a correct solution.

The exact criteria should always be defined in the corresponding rubric or teaching activity rather than inferred automatically.

## Open educational purpose

The goal of documenting the method openly is to allow educators and researchers to:

- reproduce the workflow;
- adapt it to different introductory programming contexts;
- compare human and AI-assisted feedback;
- design teacher-supervised assessment activities;
- contribute improvements and classroom evidence.

## Evidence boundary

This repository distinguishes between documented research findings and implementation choices. Numerical statistics, sample sizes, p-values, effect sizes or percentages should be reproduced only when verified from the full papers or original research datasets.
